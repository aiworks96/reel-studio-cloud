For five seconds, Earth lets go. Not a storm, not a quake. The ground stops holding the world. Then it takes it back.

The ocean breaks first. Seawater is heavy only because gravity makes it heavy. Pressure crosses water at fifteen hundred metres a second. The ocean is three point seven kilometres deep.

In two and a half seconds the pressure forgets which way is down. One point three billion cubic kilometres of water, spread evenly, is a shell two point six kilometres deep.

The sea level falls a kilometre as it spreads. Our continents average eight hundred and forty metres. Under two kilometres of water, they vanish.

The air blooms into a faint halo. Nothing flies away. Nothing falls. A dropped phone hangs in the air, waiting.

And the Earth survives. Gravity is not a wall around a planet. It is a conversation between two bodies. Cut ours, and the Sun still holds.

Which leaves five seconds to undo. One point three billion cubic kilometres of water must fall into basins that no longer fit. Every coast watches it go, then return.

So gravity is not a force we survive. It is the structure we are made of. For five seconds it was gone, and all our ground was only ever held.
